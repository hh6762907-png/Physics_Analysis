# CBSE Class XII Physics 2027 — Chapter-wise Preparation ROI

**Complete research report (Markdown edition)**  
Model v1.0.0 · research cut-off 15 November 2026 · chapter-level analysis only

> **Central question.** For an intermediate CBSE Class XII Physics student preparing for the 2027 board examination, which chapters give the greatest realistic increase in expected marks for each additional hour of preparation, and how strong is the evidence?

**Short answer.** Chapter 14 (Semiconductor Electronics) returns the most marks per additional hour, followed by Chapter 8 (Electromagnetic Waves), Chapter 13 (Nuclei), Chapter 11 (Dual Nature) and Chapter 10 (Wave Optics). Chapter 9 (Ray Optics) carries the largest expected exposure of any chapter but the longest study path, and Chapter 1 is the weakest exchange rate in the model. The top tier survives every evidence-weighting test; the exact order below rank five does not survive changes in the preparation-hour assumptions, which are the weakest input in the whole model.

**How to read the ROI column.** Incremental ROI = additional probability-adjusted marks ÷ additional preparation hours beyond the intermediate baseline. It is a marginal measure: it says where the *next* hour is best spent, not which chapters can be skipped. A chapter can be high-ROI and low-exposure (Chapter 8: 3.64 expected marks), or high-exposure and mid-ROI (Chapter 9: 8.07 expected marks, 0.28 marks per hour).

## 5. Method

1. **Exposure.** E_raw = 0.40·A + 0.35·B + 0.25·C, normalised to 70 and raked inside each official merged unit cell.
2. **Deletion.** E = E_raked × (1 − deletion share), renormalised to 70.
3. **Choice-adjusted success.** f(r) = r + 0.5 × 0.314 × (1 − r), built from the verified optional-slot share of the 2026-27 paper.
4. **Marks.** Baseline = E·f(r0); target = E·f(r1); additional = E·(f(r1) − f(r0)).
5. **Hours.** Additional hours = max(1.5, H·((r1 − r0)/r1)), H = hours to strong readiness from zero, split 62% independent / 38% guided.
6. **ROI.** Incremental = additional marks ÷ additional hours (primary). Total-mark = target marks ÷ total hours from zero (secondary).
7. **Reliability.** 100 × (0.45 evidence consistency + 0.25 source quality + 0.30 mapping certainty); grades A ≥ 74, B ≥ 64, C below.

Calibration: the intermediate profile reconstructs a 40.90/70 baseline (58.4%), and strong board readiness reconstructs 65.13/70 (93.0%).

## 9. Reliability and limitations

**Verified.** 2026-27 syllabus inventory, unit totals, paper structure, internal-choice positions, and the chapter attribution of all 33 sample-paper questions.

**Secondary.** Chapter question counts 2017–2026 and the 10-year marks aggregate (MTG). Values verified against the photographs; the publisher's attribution and inclusion rules were not re-verified against original papers.

**Expert judgement.** Preparation hours, difficulty indices, readiness profiles, the choice advantage parameter, the deletion shares and the evidence weights.

**Open limitations.** (1) No original board paper was accessible, so there is no question-level historical database, no per-set duplicate check and no per-set marks totals. (2) Compartment and supplementary papers are absent. (3) CBSE does not publish chapter-level marks, so every chapter exposure number is an estimate; only unit totals are official. (4) Hours cannot be measured from any public source. (5) MTG's predictive accuracy over time cannot be assessed because no earlier edition was available — no accuracy score is claimed. (6) The application's adaptive engine is validated by logic tests and data-integrity checks, not by real-student calibration.
